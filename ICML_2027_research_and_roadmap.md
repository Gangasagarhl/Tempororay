# Test whether memory scores survive swaps

**Commit to one measurement paper: when the language model under an agent is replaced, do the numeric usefulness scores stored on its memory items still rank those items correctly under the new model, and should they be kept, wiped, or counted at a reduced weight set from a few fresh checks?** Run it on public stream benchmarks, not on the SSD traces. Of the eight candidates it is the only one that has a dated top-venue door within reach (ICML 2027: abstract 14 January, paper 22 January 2027), a complete and costed design that one person can finish by 15 December 2026 for **about USD 700 in model calls**, no need for any customer-derived material, and a place to publish under every result pattern. The runner-up is the measurement of how much error remains behind fixed-code checks of an agent's sourced claims; it is the more open question on paper and the only candidate that fits SIGIR 2027 full papers (due 21 January 2027), where an accepted full paper is given as a talk in person, but its design is not yet worked out and SIGIR's rate is the lowest of the group at 18 percent. What is still open on 15 November 2026 is narrow and real: at least five papers have copied a scored memory to another model and all report that it helps against no memory, but none tests whether the scores still rank items correctly, and none compares keep, wipe and discount with the memory content held fixed. The honest odds are **about one in nine at ICML for a first-time sole author, about one in four after a second attempt at NeurIPS 2027, and about 60 to 75 percent for some peer-reviewed paper once the journal TMLR is the last rung**; acceptance cannot be guaranteed at any venue, and an accepted ICML or NeurIPS paper is normally a poster, not a talk. A go or no-go check falls on 22 November, after a one-week pilot. The link to the Field-to-Test Loop is thin and true: the tool never trains a model, keeps what it learns as scores beside a rented model that has already changed more than once in development, and needs a tested rule for the day that model is replaced. Four things only the employer can settle, and they should be asked on day one: publication clearance, the customer-derived column names that sit in the project's data and hand-over files, who the co-authors are, and the hours, model keys and travel.

## One candidate of eight has a dated door, a costed design and no customer data

The eight candidates were scored on two tests only, as the owner asked: the chance of acceptance at a top venue, and whether one person can finish sound experiments in 30 days. The owner's statement that the link to the project may be thin changes the ranking, because it removes the one reason to run the study on the project's own traces. Three facts from the project's files make those traces the wrong ground for the winning idea. Since 11 November 2026 the application computes the defect key and the cause in code, "never typed and never chosen by the model" (local file `13_production_app/nvme_hier/terms.py`), so inside the product a model swap cannot change what a score refers to and the effect is zero by construction. The live store holds 1 defect, 1 solution and **0 verdicts** (local file `13_production_app/db/hier_knowledge.sqlite`, counted on a copy). And the export layout of every synthetic trace was transcribed from photographs of a customer's machine (local file `13_production_app/data/lecroy_export_columns.json`), which puts a clearance question in front of any release. Public benchmarks have none of these problems.

The winning idea in its public form rests on tasks where memory is known to help and grading is done by a program. StreamBench reports gains from a simple memory of **18 to 27 points on DDXPlus diagnosis and 6 to 8 points on BIRD text-to-SQL**, with one full pass costing a few million input tokens ([StreamBench paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08747)). MemRL, the nearest accepted paper, ships the only public code that learns, stores, saves and reloads a numeric score per memory item ([MemRL README](https://github.com/MemTensor/MemRL/blob/main/README.md)), and a second group has released two frozen banks of scored memories, 679 and 1,051 items, that the owner did not train ([RoMeRL checkpoints README](https://github.com/YOUNG-fnxm/RoMeRL/blob/main/checkpoints/README.md)). The study therefore tests the scoring rules of accepted papers, on public data, with every dollar accounted for.

The key-computation split lost because its headline is already in print and its strongest arm proves nothing. A September 2026 preprint fixes the memory keys by schema and finds accuracy moves by only **+0.0004 ± 0.0020** when the writing model changes ([Goyal and Ray](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05339v1)); an August 2026 preprint moves a decision "into code" and shows it then holds on three model backbones ([Helwig](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07946)); and three more preprints keep the model out of retrieval altogether ([Eywa](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30771); [MOSS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04391); [DMF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03463)). Under a code-computed key two models agree on the key by construction, so a reviewer will call that arm true by definition. What stays unmeasured, a dose-response between key disagreement and score loss, needs four keyer arms on a public root-cause set whose supply of repeated cases per key nobody has checked, and on the owner's own traces the 38 runnable rule families found only 16 rows across four samples (local file `13_production_app/docs/OPPORTUNITY_COVERAGE_2026-11-15.md`). It survives in the plan as an optional one-page case study: the product keys by code, so the paper's prediction for the product is that scores survive.

Compile-once rules lost on novelty and on engineering risk. The idea is stated almost in the owner's words by Compiled AI, which reports break-even at about 17 uses and 57 times fewer tokens at 1,000 ([Compiled AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05150)), by Argos for anomaly rules ([Argos](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14170v1)) and by CodeAD for log rules at USD 0.07 per rule ([CodeAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22986v1)); a database paper measured 110 times fewer tokens in 2023 ([Evaporate](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol17/p92-arora.pdf)). The parts still open, how repeatable the compile step is and how compiled rules fail after a format change, depend on the project's code-writing agent family, which has run with a real model exactly once (652 seconds, 24 model calls, three faults since repaired and re-tested only with a scripted model) and has never coded one of the 38 rule families (local file `research_notes/Any CSV coverage by agent families/E_real_model_run_on_unknown_csv.md`; local file `13_production_app/docs/OPPORTUNITY_COVERAGE_2026-11-15.md`). The audit of the built work estimates 12 to 16 days of new code before the first table, on traces in the customer's layout.

Sourced column meanings lost because the design is no longer alone and the venues are wrong. One preprint already has a code profiler, a model that only proposes, an evidence class on every meaning, a human acceptance step, a downstream correctness measure and a model swap, and reports 0.475 accuracy on the 42 percent of columns it commits to against 0.223 for the plain model ([Helwig](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07946)). The remaining gap (real manuals as sources, quote-support scoring, very wide tables) fits a database venue such as PVLDB; the literature note found no evidence that SIGIR, ICML or NeurIPS main tracks publish it, and the test data would be the customer-derived headers.

The trace benchmark lost for this deadline, not for good. Eleven searches across two passes found no public PCIe or NVMe trace set with labelled faults, but the label that was to be its selling point, the causal chain, is already claimed for cloud telemetry ([OpenRCA 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27154)), frontier agents now reach 90.0 percent top-1 accuracy on one such benchmark ([trajectory study on RCABench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21310v1)), and a paper just accepted at FSE 2026 argues that when simple rules match the models the benchmark is too easy ([RCABench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04711)), while the owner's own rule engine finds 437 of 485 injected errors (local file `10_defect_isolation_engine/REPORT_defect_isolation.md`). KDD's datasets track demands that "Synthetic data must be accompanied with a quantification and a discussion of its representativeness" ([KDD 2027 datasets and benchmarks call](https://kdd2027.kdd.org/datasets-and-benchmarks-track-call-for-papers/)), and the best-matched track, NeurIPS Evaluations and Datasets, opens about May 2027 and requires code and hosted data at submission. If the employer clears the generator under a neutral column layout, this is the natural second paper.

The last two cannot produce an honest table in 30 days. The replay result of "22 of 22 scripts" was made with a stand-in and a fixed template, the simulated drive's fault is built from the plan being tested, and without the drive's time hint the search did worse than random, 33 of 50 against 37 of 50 (local file `13_production_app/docs/OPPORTUNITY_COVERAGE_2026-11-15.md`). The four-verdict graph has no real verdicts, and the project's own measurement found that carried counts alone did not help: 26 better, 18 equal, 16 worse (same file).

### The runner-up: what share of "verified" claims are still false

The runner-up asks one question with a known answer sheet: of the claims an agent makes that pass a fixed-code check (the attached query, re-run, returns the stated value; the attached quote is found at the stated file and page), what share are still false, and of the claims that fail, what share were true? The designs exist but their measured behaviour is almost unreported: one preprint that marks only checked numbers as verified reports no experiment ([Proof-Carrying Numbers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06902)), and another that gates claims on recorded runs has eight tasks ([EviBound](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05524)). Neighbouring measurements say the leftover error is not small: only 74.5 percent of citations in generative search engines support their sentence ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848)), up to 57 percent of citations lack faithfulness in a paper from a SIGIR conference ([Wallat et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18004)), and citations to the wrong page of the right legal case are caught only 37 to 61 percent of the time ([Verma](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12571)). SIGIR 2027 names "Measuring groundedness, hallucination, and factual accuracy in generative retrieval." among its evaluation topics ([SIGIR 2027 full-paper call](https://sigir2027.org/pages/submit-full.html)).

It is second, not first, for four reasons. No pilot, call count or budget exists for it. Its public ground would be labelled claim sets such as FEVEROUS, and nobody has checked that those claims are a fair stand-in for claims an agent makes. The project's own evidence is thin: the live evidence table holds 150 claims, of which 26 have a document source and all 26 passed, 124 have no checkable source, and none failed a check (local file `13_production_app/db/agentic.sqlite`, counted on a copy), so the share of errors a check catches could be near zero. And SIGIR accepted 234 of 1,271 full papers in 2026, 18 percent, lists "Clear lack of topical fit for SIGIR." as a ground for rejection without review, and requires an author to serve as a reviewer ([SIGIR 2027 full-paper call](https://sigir2027.org/pages/submit-full.html); [SIGIR 2026 accepted papers](https://sigir2026.org/en-AU/pages/program/accepted-papers); [ACM Digital Library, SIGIR '26](https://dl.acm.org/doi/proceedings/10.1145/3805712)). Switch to it only if the first idea fails its gate twice, as set out in the roadmap below.

## Five papers moved scored memory across models; none checked that the scores still rank

Reviewers value this paper because the gap can be checked in a minute against papers they already accepted, and because the method under test assumes the thing the paper varies. MemRL, a NeurIPS 2026 poster, gave a memory bank built by one model to three others and saw scores rise from 0.150 to 0.531, 0.347 to 0.583 and 0.354 to 0.571; it reads this as "suggesting that the learned utility over memories generalizes beyond any single model's inherent strength", yet its transfer table has no run with scores wiped, no column for a memory the target built itself and no repeated runs, and its own stability analysis lists "Frozen Inference Policy" as its first assumption ([MemRL v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03192v2)). A stored score is an estimate of reward under one fixed model; after a swap, by the paper's own definition, the quantity it estimates has changed. That gives the introduction its opening line.

The practical motive is equally easy to verify. Anthropic gives "at least 60 days' notice before model retirement", retired seven model versions between January and August 2026 and retires another on 30 November 2026 ([Anthropic model deprecations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations)). OpenAI shuts down the GPT-5 family on 11 December 2026 and the GPT-4o family on 14 January 2027 ([OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)), which means MemRL's own tables cannot be re-run on their original models after the ICML deadline. Azure returns `410 Gone` for a model 18 months after launch and states "Retirement dates aren't extendable." ([Foundry Models lifecycle policy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/openai/concepts/model-retirements)). Any team that keeps scored memory beside a rented model faces the keep-or-wipe decision about once a year, and the official NeurIPS 2026 list shows the appetite: 14 agent-memory evaluation papers in one track, none of them, by a title search, about what happens to scored memory when the model is replaced ([NeurIPS 2026 accepted-papers data](https://neurips.cc/static/virtual/data/neurips-2026-orals-posters.json)).

### What exactly is still open on 15 November 2026

Four things were not found in 38 arXiv queries, 28 OpenReview term searches, the 139 papers that cite MemRL and the titles of 9,276 NeurIPS 2026 entries. Nobody has tested, against a ground truth measured under the new model, whether scores earned under the old model still rank memory items correctly. Nobody has compared keeping the scores, wiping them, discounting them by a fixed factor and discounting them by a factor set from a few fresh checks, with the memory content held fixed, over many ordered model pairs. Nobody has counted the fresh episodes needed to recover. And nobody has split the answer by who assigned the credit: a program's reward, as in MemRL, or the model's own judgement of what helped, as in the counters of ACE ([ACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618)).

The gap is about one experiment wide and several groups stand one table away from it. Searches surfaced nearly forty related works posted in 2026, about fifteen of them in September alone, the ICLR 2027 submission month, and ICLR 2027 decisions arrive on **16 December 2026**, the day after the planned experimental freeze ([ICLR 2027 dates](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/Dates)). The largest unverified risk is that the bodies of three ICLR 2027 submissions could not be read by script, because OpenReview returned a challenge page; only abstracts, and the code of one, were read. They must be read through a normal browser in the first week.

Five claims must not appear in the paper as new: that scored memory has been moved across models (five times), that own memory has been compared with transferred memory, that a few fresh local trials have been compared with carried records (one submission, at small scale), that fixed or code-made keys survive a swap, and that the discount rule is a method. The rule is a power prior, in which old evidence is raised to a weight between 0 and 1 ([Ibrahim and Chen](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistical-science/volume-15/issue-1/Power-prior-distributions-for-regression-models/10.1214/ss/1009212673.full)), with the weight chosen from fresh data as in clinical-trial statistics ([Gravestock and Held](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08239)). **The contribution is the measurement, the tested recipe and the released outcome tables.** Because the nearest preprint is titled "Does Your Agent's Memory Survive a Model Upgrade?", the paper should speak of scores, not memory, and because every score in the study comes from a program's grade or the model's own marking, the word "human" must stay out of the title and abstract.

The first target is the ICML 2027 main track with the contribution type "Datasets, Evaluations and Benchmarks", which is chosen "when the primary contribution advances the resources, methods, or scientific understanding used to evaluate machine learning systems", lists "auditing or stress testing machine learning systems" among its examples and "does **not** need to introduce a new learning algorithm" ([ICML 2027 Contribution Types](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2027/ContributionTypes)). If the December result is a large drop with a working recipe, the "General" type fits better; the "Negative Results" type carries a stated high bar and should be avoided unless the result is a clear, surprising failure of kept scores. ## Thirty days, about USD 700, and a go or no-go on 22 November

### The design: force each memory item into the prompt and score every rule offline

The study uses a thin score layer of about 300 lines that implements two published rules unchanged: MemRL's update (step 0.3, starting value 0, equal weight on similarity and score, as in its [released configuration](https://github.com/MemTensor/MemRL/blob/main/configs/rl_hle_config.yaml)) and ACE's helpful and harmful counters. The main ground is two single-call streams from StreamBench, DDXPlus (1,764 items) and BIRD (1,534 items); one confirming leg runs MemRL's released code on LifelongAgentBench operating-system tasks (corrected on 15 November after verification: the database tasks are near ceiling for current models, with base scores of 93.0 to 96.2 in RoMeRL's Table 3, so they cannot show a score effect), and a nearly free extra tests the two frozen RoMeRL score banks under new models. Running MemRL, CHIME or RoMeRL end to end on their own benchmarks is out of reach: MemRL's maintainers report that one benchmark alone costs "close to USD 1,000" and takes days ([MemRL issue 10](https://github.com/MemTensor/MemRL/issues/10)), CHIME has released no code, and RoMeRL released evaluation code only.

Six models give twelve ordered swaps: three same-maker upgrades (Claude Haiku 4.5 to Haiku 5.5, GPT-5.6-luna to GPT-6-luna, and the open-weight Qwen3.5-27B to Qwen3.8-27B), their three reverses as a downgrade control, and the six cross-maker pairs among the three newer models. List prices run from USD 0.10 to 1.00 per million input tokens ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing); [OpenRouter models API](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models)). The Qwen pair carries Apache-2.0 weights ([Qwen3.8-27B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B)) and is the anchor that others can re-run after the closed models retire. Nothing in the design relies on fixed sampling, because current Claude models reject a temperature setting ([Anthropic model deprecations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations)) and identical runs of "deterministic" models have differed by up to 15 percent in accuracy ([Atil et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04667)); every cell is repeated and the same-model disagreement is measured directly.

Each stream is shuffled with a recorded seed and cut into three parts that share no task: about 600 tasks on which the source model earns scores, about 200 probe tasks, and about 300 final test tasks. During building, the item shown is chosen at random among the top candidates 30 percent of the time, because in MemRL's released bank most scores never move (33,463 of about 35,185 items sat at exactly zero in the maintainer's own run) and retrieval there has no exploration ([MemRL issue 7](https://github.com/MemTensor/MemRL/issues/7); [MemRL issue 11](https://github.com/MemTensor/MemRL/issues/11)). The core instrument is the probe table: for every probe task and each of its top four candidate items, the target model is run twice with exactly that one item shown, and also with no item. Because the table holds the outcome of every candidate, the choice any rule would make can be scored by look-up, with no further calls.

| Arm on the day of the swap | Memory content | Scores |
|---|---|---|
| No memory | None | None |
| Own | The target's own bank, built on the same stream | The target's own |
| Keep | The source's bank | Unchanged |
| Wipe | The source's bank | Reset, so ranking is by similarity alone |
| Fixed discount | The source's bank | Multiplied by 0.5, with a sweep from 0.1 to 0.9 |
| Calibrated discount | The source's bank | Weight chosen from 8, 16, 32 or 64 fresh checks |
| Re-scored (ceiling) | The source's bank | Re-earned by the target on the probe pool |

The headline number is retained validity. Within each probe task, take pairs of candidates whose outcomes differ and ask how often the one with the higher carried score did better; 0.5 is chance. Measure this at home (source scores against source outcomes) and after the swap (source scores against target outcomes), and report (swap − 0.5) divided by (home − 0.5). Secondary numbers are the success rate of the item each arm would show first, task success on the final test set, the number of fresh episodes needed to reach 95 percent of the ceiling, and cost. The measure is not circular: the tasks that judge a score never helped earn it, the experimenter and not the score chooses which item is shown, and the grade is a program's (a diagnosis label match or SQL execution). Three controls set the noise floor: a self-swap of each model onto itself with fresh repeats, repeat disagreement on identical cells, and two independently built banks of the same model. A drop is claimed only if it exceeds the self-swap drop with an interval that excludes zero.

Three predictions turn twelve pairs into a finding. Retained validity should rise with the chance-corrected agreement between the two models' outcomes on the same task-and-item cells. The gap between keep and wipe should shrink as the target's no-memory success rises. And scores assigned by the model itself should lose more validity than scores earned from a program's reward. The third is the single most publishable outcome whatever the average drop, because it hands a practitioner a rule and a reason. The predictions, the four primary contrasts, the margins (5 points for a difference, 3 points either way for "no drop", tested as equivalence and not inferred from a failed difference test) and the go or no-go rule go into a dated file before the first paid run. Analysis is paired on the same probe tasks, with intervals from a bootstrap over tasks and stream orders, a correction across the four primary contrasts, and every pair shown in a table, following the standard practice for model evaluations ([Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)). One limit should be stated in the paper's first page: the probe shows one item at a time, while MemRL shows several, so interactions between items appear only in the smaller live runs.

### Cost and the gate after week one

One benchmark at the recommended size is about 140,400 calls; at an assumed 2,500 input and 500 output tokens per call that is about USD 243 at list price across the six models, with the older Haiku 4.5 alone accounting for USD 117. Two benchmarks plus the small released-code leg, with 30 percent added for the pilot, debugging and re-runs, come to **about USD 700**, or about USD 1,400 if real token use is double the assumption; a cut-down version with one benchmark and four cheap models is about USD 100, and the largest sensible version is USD 2,600 to 3,000. These are estimates built on assumed token counts; the pilot measures the real figure on its fifth day. Anthropic's entry tier caps spending at USD 500 a month, which the recommended size stays under ([Anthropic rate limits](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits)). API time is about 33 hours at modest concurrency and the whole computation about four to five machine days. The binding resource is one person's working time for four weeks, not money.

The pilot runs DDXPlus on three models (Haiku 4.5, Haiku 5.5, Qwen3.8-27B) with one stream order, 300 build tasks and 100 probe tasks. **On 22 November the full grid goes ahead only if all five conditions hold**: the source model with its own memory beats no memory by at least 5 points with an interval that excludes zero; the scores mean something at home, a concordance of at least 0.60 with a lower bound above 0.55 for at least one of the two score types; same-model repeat disagreement on identical cells is below 15 percent; at least 150 items have five or more visits and at least 30 percent of probe tasks have candidates whose outcomes differ; and the measured cost per 1,000 calls is within twice the estimate. If memory does not help on DDXPlus, switch to ToolBench or Spider before spending more. If the scores are no better than chance at home, raise exploration in the build and re-pilot for two days. If the gate fails a second time, the January top-venue attempt with this idea is over, and two honest choices remain: write the finding that greedy retrieval leaves stored scores uninformative even before a swap, for TMLR or the NeurIPS track, or move to the runner-up on a February-to-May timetable. If the gate passes but the first cross pair shows no drop beyond the self-swap noise, continue: "no drop" is a claim only after the full grid and an equivalence test.

### The conditions reviewers demand, and how the design meets them

Published reviews of accepted memory papers ask for the same things by name: "many main experiments fix the task agent to GPT 5.1 mini, so it is hard to judge robustness across base models" and "With small evaluation sets, performance may also be sensitive to decoding randomness or sampling seeds" ([published reviews of MemoryArena](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~jmcauley/reviews/icml26b.pdf)); reviewers of another accepted benchmark asked for a no-memory run and for the API cost ([published reviews of MemoryAgentBench](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~jmcauley/reviews/iclr26c.pdf)). Meeting the conditions removes the reasons for a clear rejection; it does not remove chance.

## The question fell out of one design rule: never train, keep scores beside a rented model

The idea is a consequence of how the project chose to deliver the "RL" in its funded title, "RL-Powered Field-to-Test Loop" (local file `13_production_app/docs/OPPORTUNITY_STATEMENT.md`). The first research report, on 25 October 2026, concluded that "Bandit-weighted case memory can replace the RL agent" and described the team's design as a weighted graph with admin verdicts (local file `11_failure_reproduction_research/RL style failure reproduction without training - research report.md`). The simulation written that same night already contained a small version of today's question with firmware in place of the model: 20 percent of fault cards changed their dominant cause at a "firmware change" (local file `12_feedback_graph_simulation/simulate_feedback_graph.py`). On 2 November the owner fixed exactly four feedback signals (correct, correct with edits, wrong, wrong with edits; local file `13_production_app/nvme_hier/defect_graph.py`); on 8 November the plan for approval set down "No training" as a principle and put the agent runtime "behind a swappable interface" (local file `Approach_plan_for_approval_2026-11-08.md`); on 9 November the owner recorded that reinforcement learning is "MIMICKED on procedural graphs". From that point the scores on the graph were the only thing the system learns, and the model under them was a rented part that could be exchanged.

The question itself was first written down on 10 November 2026, in a novelty review the owner commissioned for a paper and a manager pitch. That review found thirteen attractive claims about the graph formula already published by others and one gap with no direct hit: "no study in these notes measures whether a procedure's accumulated weight still predicts its quality after the LLM is upgraded", adding that this gap "is not where the project started" (local file `reports/Reinforced memory and harness research gaps.md`). The honest account is therefore that the research round raised the question and the design made it unavoidable. Two days later a second round, limited to one person and frozen models, selected the same idea again on feasibility.

Three things then changed its form, all from the project's own work. On 11 November the application moved the defect key and the cause into code, so the earlier picture of a new model "filing faults on different pages" stopped describing the product; an older prompt in which the model picks the cause from a fixed list still sits in the code with no caller (local file `13_production_app/nvme_hier/isolate.py`). On 15 November the project measured, on its simulated drive, that stored history helped when it matched (better in 28 of 30 cases) and hurt when stale (worse in 25 of 30), its own synthetic evidence that carried evidence misleads when the thing underneath changes (local file `13_production_app/docs/OPPORTUNITY_COVERAGE_2026-11-15.md`). And a literature recheck the same day found that five papers, not one, had already copied scored memory across models, and that the released code for the nearest of them is too costly to re-run in full. The result is the present form: the general question, on public tasks, with the product as a one-page illustration of the design that should be safe. In three weeks of development the tool itself has run under at least three different models (Claude Haiku 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and a free DeepSeek model through a terminal agent), its configured model changed from Haiku to Sonnet between 12 and 15 November (local file `13_production_app/config/app_config.json`), and its saved explanations are already keyed by model id (local file `13_production_app/app/api/isolate_api.py`), so the swap is an observed event in this project, not a thought experiment.

## The account for the manager: a thin, true link and four things only the employer can settle

The short account, every sentence of which the files support, is this:

> Our tool never trains a model. What it learns is kept as scores on a graph, beside a language model we rent, and vendors retire such models about 12 to 18 months after release on as little as 60 days' notice; during development the model under our tool has already changed more than once. Published work shows that scored memory still helps after the model is replaced, but nobody has tested whether the scores themselves are still right or what to do with them. I measured that on public benchmarks, with public data and about USD 700 of model calls, and wrote it up for ICML 2027. The paper uses no company or customer data. It does not advance the project's sixteen deliverables. What the project gets from it is a repeatable check to run at every model change, and evidence for or against our decision to keep the model out of the keys.

The link is thin by design and should be called thin. The paper studies scores earned from a program's grade on public tasks; the product's scores are meant to be engineers' verdicts, and the store holds none yet. The paper's prediction for the product is the unexciting one, that scores survive because code computes the keys, and the optional case study either agrees or finds where a model-made decision leaks in. The model's remaining influence in the shipped flow is bounded and known: column meanings, rule code written once and approved, the recorded rows kept in a test sequence, and free text.

What the organisation gains is modest and concrete. First, an upgrade check: the pieces the study has to build (a model field on stored items, a token and cost log, a cache that respects repeats, detection of a silent fall-back to a stand-in model) are gaps the project's own audit already lists, and vendors themselves advise "thorough testing of your applications with the new models well before the retirement date" ([Anthropic model deprecations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations)). Second, a governance answer: NIST's generative-AI profile asks organisations to "Re-assess model risks" for third-party models and to consider "GAI system changes over time (e.g., fine-tuning, drift, decay)" ([NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)), though no framework found literally requires re-validation after a vendor swap, and an internal debug assistant is very probably not a high-risk system under the EU AI Act. Third, standing: peers are publishing agent work on debug and failure analysis, among them NVIDIA ([FVDebug](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15906)) and Solidigm, whose debug agent it calls "central to Solidigm's customer issue resolution strategy" ([Solidigm](https://www.solidigm.com/products/technology/solidigm-ai-innovation-internal-excellence.html)), and one study of job offers found that firms which let scientists publish "extract, on average, a 25% wage discount" ([Stern, NBER w7410](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7410)). The evidence that publishing raises firm value is mixed and should be presented as such ([Arora, Belenzon and Patacconi](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/stratm/v39y2018i1p3-32.html)). Fourth, an accepted paper at ICML, NeurIPS or TMLR makes the author an eligible reviewer, which lifts the next paper, the one about the tool or its benchmark, out of the group with no eligible reviewer ([ICML 2027 quotas](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2027/ReciprocalReviewingAndSubmissionQuotas)).

Four matters are outside any plan the owner can make alone. **Publication clearance** comes first: a preprint, a posted abstract, an anonymised code repository and a submission are each a public disclosure, and outside the United States and Japan there is in general no grace period for patents after the inventor's own disclosure ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/uk/patent/672370/patent-grace-period-opportunity-or-trap)). The request should cover the paper, the experiment code, the use of the employer's name as affiliation, and whether an invention disclosure is wanted first; the discount rule is known statistics, so a paper is also a cheap defensive publication, but that judgement belongs to counsel.

**Customer-derived material** is the second, and it matters even though the committed paper uses none of it. The project's column lists were transcribed from 25 screen photos of a customer's Windows machine, and the file that holds them records the customer's file path, trace name and analyzer version (local file `13_production_app/data/lecroy_export_columns.json`); the generator writes every synthetic recording "in the customer's exact column layout" (local file `13_production_app/data/generator/export_levels.py`); one upload's metadata carries a device-maker's name typed by an operator; the reference library reproduces 615 one-line quotes from vendor manuals and the NVMe specifications; the application contains an agent built to read a decompiled copy of the analyzer's program; and no file states who wrote the 485-card fault atlas or under what terms. A hand-over folder prepared for a GitHub repository on 15 November contains an archive of those real header names (local file `_github_copy_2026-11-15/lecroy_export_column_names.zip`); whether that repository is private should be confirmed before anything else is published. Until the employer answers, the optional SSD case study stays out of the paper, or appears as a description with no data, and no trace, generator or layout is released.

**Co-authors** are the third. The paper as planned has one author with no earlier publications, the profile that ICLR's own data show accepted at half the rate. A co-author with one accepted paper at ICML, NeurIPS, ICLR, KDD, AAAI, an ACL venue or TMLR makes the team eligible under ICML's rule, at the price of a reviewing duty; this is the largest single lever on the odds that does not involve the experiment, though the ICLR figure is a group average over a pool the chairs say contains much low-quality work, so the size of the benefit is unknown. Whether a manager, a colleague or an outside statistician is named is the employer's decision, authorship must reflect real contribution, and the author list has to be settled before the abstract deadline of 14 January. No file names a candidate.

The fourth is resources that are not the owner's to grant: working hours for the experiment and the writing, the model keys and budget (no file says whether the company's model service offers the six models), travel to Rio or Europe for an in-person presentation, and whether two or three engineers can be lent later for real verdicts.

## Conclusion

The owner's release from a tight project link is what made the choice clear. The project's best design decision, keeping the model out of the keys, is exactly what makes its own traces a poor ground for the paper, since the effect there is zero by construction; the general question it raised is best answered on public tasks, where the cost is small, the data are clean of customer material and every result has a home. The same decision becomes the paper's quiet punchline: a system built this way should be safe at a model change, and here is the measurement of what happens to systems that are not.

The actions that most change the odds need no computing time: the clearance request and the co-author question on day one, the reading of three unread submissions in week one, and a prediction file dated before the first paid call. Three unknowns remain and no planning removes them: the size of the effect, which the pilot begins to show on 22 November; what the ICLR 2027 decisions of 16 December add to the neighbours; and the employer's answers. The owner should plan for a poster, not a talk, at the first accepted venue, treat a second submission as the normal path, and keep the trace benchmark and the checked-claims study as the second and third papers that an accepted first one makes easier.
